# Skill 01: Targeted Job Outreach & Positioning

**Category:** Job Search  
**Workflow:** Research → Position → Pitch → Follow Up

## Objective

Research job opportunities and create concise, personalized, evidence-backed applications and outreach messages. Make it easy for a hiring manager to understand who I am, why I fit the opportunity, and what I can contribute.

## When to Use

- Applying to a specific job opening
- Cold DMing founders, hiring managers, or recruiters
- Reaching out to companies without advertised openings
- Tailoring a job application or cover letter
- Following up after an application

## Workflow

### 1. Research the Opportunity

Analyze the company's product, market, target customers, business model, recent developments, and role requirements. Identify what the team is likely trying to accomplish and any relevant challenges.

**Output:** 2–3 useful insights about the company and its needs. Distinguish verified facts from hypotheses.

### 2. Identify the Value Overlap

Match the company's needs to my actual experience. Select the one or two most relevant achievements, domain-specific strengths, or examples of delivered work instead of summarizing my entire CV.

**Output:** A clear answer to *Why am I a strong fit for this particular opportunity?*

### 3. Build an Evidence-Backed Pitch

Answer three questions:

1. Who am I?
2. Why am I relevant to this company and role?
3. What can I contribute?

For initial DMs, aim for **3–6 sentences**. Lead with concrete evidence and outcomes where available.

**Output:** A concise draft with a clear next step.

### 4. Personalize and Deliver

Tailor the opening, proof points, tone, and call to action to the company, role, recipient, and channel. A founder DM should read differently from a formal application.

**Output:** A natural, ready-to-send message that does not feel mass-produced.

### 5. Follow Up and Evaluate

If there is no response, follow up politely once after a reasonable interval. Track outreach and responses. Assess whether the opportunity meets my own requirements, rather than treating every reply as a win.

**Output:** A recommendation to advance, follow up, or move on.

## Core Principles

- **Concise:** Initial DMs are usually 3–6 sentences, not personal autobiographies.
- **Relevant:** Put the most role-specific information first.
- **Value-driven:** Explain what I have done and what I could contribute, not only what I hope to learn.
- **Personalized:** Demonstrate genuine knowledge of the company and opportunity.
- **Evidence-based:** Show, don't just tell. Quantify outcomes where accurate.
- **Honest:** Never invent experience, results, company research, or relationships.
- **Efficient:** Avoid extensive speculative unpaid projects before mutual interest exists.
- **Two-way fit:** Evaluate the employer's expectations, culture, compensation, and working arrangement as well as my own candidacy.

## Default Output for Each Opportunity

1. **Company and role assessment:** What the company does, what the role needs, and any concerns.
2. **Top 2–3 fit signals:** The most relevant evidence from my background.
3. **Positioning angle:** The single strongest story to lead with.
4. **Ready-to-send outreach:** A concise message appropriate for the recipient and channel.
5. **Next action:** Apply, DM, ask a question, follow up, or pass.

**Important:** Evaluate whether the opportunity is worth pursuing before drafting outreach. Ask for missing essential context rather than inventing details.
